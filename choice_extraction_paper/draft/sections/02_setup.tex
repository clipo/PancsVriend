\section{Setup and estimators}
\label{sec:setup}

The target is the distribution the deployed sampler draws from, not the model's ``belief''.
An agent's reply is constrained by a GBNF grammar whose language $L$ is whitespace followed by MOVE or STAY, in any casing.
Let $s$ be the text generated so far (a prefix) and $z(t\mid s)$ the logit of token $t$.
Sampling at temperature $T$, with top-$k$, top-$p$ and min-$p$ switched off, gives the tempered distribution
\begin{equation}
p_T(t\mid s)=\frac{\exp\big(z(t\mid s)/T\big)}{\sum_{u}\exp\big(z(u\mid s)/T\big)}.
\end{equation}
The allowed set $A(s)$ holds the tokens $t$ for which $s$ followed by $t$ is still a prefix of some string in $L$.
llama.cpp enforces the grammar by rejection: it draws a token and resamples only if the draw is invalid.
The realised next-token law is therefore the tempered distribution renormalised over $A(s)$:
\begin{equation}
q(t\mid s)=\frac{p_T(t\mid s)}{\sum_{u\in A(s)}p_T(u\mid s)},\qquad t\in A(s).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Path sum.}
Every reply that spells option $o$ is a path of tokens through the prefix tree.
Its probability is the product of $q$ along the path, and the choice probability is the sum over all paths that end in $o$:
\begin{equation}
\Ppath(o)=\sum_{\pi\to o}\;\prod_{k=1}^{|\pi|}q(t_k\mid s_{k-1}).
\end{equation}
Because $L$ is finite apart from leading whitespace, the tree is finite once whitespace runs are cut off.
We expand states in order of mass.
Each state costs one request that returns the top $n=64$ tempered probabilities.
We stop at depth 8 or when a state's mass falls below $10^{-7}$.
All mass dropped by these cut-offs, plus mass outside the top 64, is added to a bound $B$, and the true value lies within $B$ of the estimate.
The interval is two-sided, because allowed tokens outside the top 64 would also enlarge the denominator of $q$, so the estimate can overshoot as well as undershoot:
\begin{equation}
\big|\,\Ppath(o)-\hat P_{\text{path}}(o)\,\big|\;\le\;B.
\end{equation}
The largest bound we observe, $1.19\times10^{-7}$, equals $2^{-23}$, so the precision is that of the single-precision probabilities the server returns.

\paragraph{First-token readouts.}
We compare two shortcuts. FT-mask corresponds to the restricted first-token readout of \citet{ahnert2026survey}.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{Canonical first token} ($\pcanon$): the softmax at temperature 1 over the two canonical tokens for MOVE and STAY at the empty prefix, renormalised over the pair. We also report it at the serving temperature $T=0.3$.
  \item \emph{Masked first token} ($\pmask$): $q$ at the empty prefix, summed over the allowed tokens whose first non-whitespace letter is M (or S), renormalised over all such resolving tokens.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{When is the first token enough?}
In this grammar no token can start both MOVE and STAY, so the first non-whitespace letter decides the answer.
Let $\moff$ be the share of the allowed first-token mass, under $q$, that falls on pure-whitespace tokens, and $a$ the probability of MOVE given that the reply starts with whitespace. Then
\begin{equation}
\Ppath(\text{MOVE})-\pmask=\moff\,\big(a-\pmask\big),\qquad
\big|\Ppath-\pmask\big|\le\moff .
\end{equation}
A masked first-token read is therefore exact up to $\moff$, and the path sum is what tells us $\moff$.
FT-canon has no such guarantee: it ignores casing and spacing variants and is read at the wrong temperature.

\paragraph{What this is not.}
The path sum is the law of the sampler with local masking.
It is not the model's probability conditioned on the whole reply lying in $L$, which renormalises globally and can differ \citep{park2024grammar}.
We measure that gap in \S\ref{sec:shortcuts}.

\paragraph{Assumptions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{Text prefixes.} Each prefix is sent as assistant text and re-tokenised by the server. Token paths that spell the same text are therefore merged, and the next-token probabilities are those at the server's tokenisation of that text, which need not be the tokens the sampler drew. \tbd{compare text-level and token-level prefixes on a few states.}
  \item \emph{Stopping.} Once a complete option is generated, the grammar allows only end-of-sequence, so stopping has probability 1 under local masking.
  \item \emph{Reply length.} The live agent stops after 5 tokens while the tree goes to depth 8. Paths of 6 to 8 tokens count in the path sum but would be unparseable, and dropped, when sampled. \tbd{report their total mass.}
\end{itemize}
