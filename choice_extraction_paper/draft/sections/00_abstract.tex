\begin{abstract}
Simulations built from LLM agents need the probability of each agent's choice in each state.
In practice that probability is read from the first answer token's logits or estimated from hundreds of sampled replies.
First-token reads are known to disagree with what models actually answer, and sampled estimates are noisy.
We show that sampled estimates are also sensitive to how the model is served.
For a decision with a finite set of options and a grammar-constrained sampler, we compute the exact choice probability the sampler realises.
We sum the locally renormalised probability of every grammar-valid token path that spells each option, and bound the mass left out.
On 9 open-weight models (four quantisation levels) and 540 decision states per model from a Schelling segregation simulation, the path sum matches a sequential census of 100 sampled replies per state (goodness-of-fit $p$ from 0.27 to 0.94 in the first census; \tbd{re-census values}).
It needs 1.8 to 2.8 requests per state, and the reported bound on omitted mass is at single-precision level, at most $1.2\times10^{-7}$.
First-token readouts deviate from it by \tbd{E1}.
With four requests in flight, the probability the server returns for the same request varies by up to 1.00 on near-tie states, and a date printed by the chat template moves it by up to 0.45 across days.
We release the extractor and a checklist for reporting choice probabilities from LLM agents.
\end{abstract}
