\section{Validation against a sampled census}
\label{sec:validation}

In the first census the path sum agrees with sequential sampling on all nine models (Table~\ref{tab:census}); a re-census after a race-label fix has passed the goodness-of-fit test on seven models so far.

\paragraph{Setting.}
Agents in a Sakoda--Schelling simulation see their neighbourhood composition and answer MOVE or STAY.
Each model has 540 decision states (45 compositions $\times$ 2 roles $\times$ 6 scenarios) and is served by llama.cpp (build b1-a4ce259) through its chat endpoint, at $T=0.3$, one request at a time, with prompt caching off.
The probabilities are read from the same endpoint and payload the simulation samples from, so template rendering is identical by construction.

\paragraph{Census.}
For every state we draw 100 replies one at a time (54,000 per model).
We test the whole table at once with the deviance $G$ of the census counts against the path-sum probabilities, and calibrate $G$ with a parametric bootstrap from the exact table (10,000 replicates).
Because the test covers all states, no cell is selected after the fact.
The census tests the final answer, so when $\moff$ is small it validates the path sum as a whole but cannot separately test its multi-token paths (E3 in \S\ref{sec:shortcuts}).
Figure~3 plots the path sum against the census MOVE rate with binomial intervals. \tbd{Figure 3.}

\paragraph{Mass closure and reproducibility.}
Over all stored states, $P(\text{MOVE})+P(\text{STAY})$ differs from 1 by at most $9.9\times10^{-8}$.
Re-running the extraction reproduces eight models bit for bit and Hermes within $10^{-9}$.
